Section A — Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. A bag contains 5 red balls and 3 blue balls. One ball is picked without looking. What is the probability of getting a red ball?

✓ Correct Answer: 5/8. There are 5 favourable outcomes out of 8 total balls.

Q2. A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting tails?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/2. The two equally likely outcomes are heads and tails.

Q3. A standard die is rolled once. What is the probability of getting an even number?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/2. Even outcomes are 2, 4 and 6: 3 out of 6.

Q4. Which probability represents an event that is certain to happen?

✓ Correct Answer: 1. A certain event has probability 1.

Q5. A box contains 7 green counters and 1 yellow counter. What is the probability of selecting the yellow counter?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/8. There is 1 yellow counter among 8 counters.

Q6. A die has faces numbered 1 to 6. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/3. The favourable numbers are 5 and 6: 2/6 = 1/3.

Q7. A spinner has 4 equal sections numbered 1, 2, 3 and 4. What is the probability of landing on an odd number?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/2. The odd sections are 1 and 3, giving 2 out of 4.

Q8. A bag contains only blue balls. What is the probability of drawing a red ball?

✓ Correct Answer: 0. Drawing a red ball is impossible because there are no red balls.

Q9. A fair die is rolled. What is the probability of getting a number less than 3?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/3. Numbers less than 3 are 1 and 2: 2/6 = 1/3.

Q10. A class survey records favourite colours: 12 students choose blue, 8 choose green and 5 choose yellow. If one student is selected at random, what is the probability that the student chose green?

✓ Correct Answer: 8/25. Total students = 12 + 8 + 5 = 25.

Q11. A coin is tossed 20 times and shows heads 13 times. What is the experimental probability of heads?

✓ Correct Answer: 13/20. Experimental probability = number of times event occurs ÷ total trials.

Q12. A bag contains 4 white, 6 black and 5 red balls. What is the probability of selecting a ball that is not black?

✓ Correct Answer: 3/5. Non-black balls = 4 + 5 = 9, total = 15, so 9/15 = 3/5.

Q13. Which of the following is an impossible event when a normal six-sided die is rolled?

✓ Correct Answer: Getting 9. A standard die has only numbers 1 to 6.

Q14. A spinner has 10 equal sections, of which 4 are red. What is the probability of not landing on red?

✓ Correct Answer: 3/5. Non-red sections = 6 out of 10 = 3/5.

Q15. A number card is chosen from cards numbered 1 to 10. What is the probability of choosing a multiple of 3?

✓ Correct Answer: 3/10. Multiples of 3 are 3, 6 and 9: 3 favourable cards out of 10.

Section B — True / False

Q16. The probability of an impossible event is 0.

✓ Correct Answer: True.

Q17. The probability of a certain event is 0.

✓ Correct Answer: False. A certain event has probability 1.

Q18. A probability can never be greater than 1.

✓ Correct Answer: True.

Q19. For equally likely outcomes, probability is calculated as favourable outcomes divided by total outcomes.

✓ Correct Answer: True.

Q20. When a fair coin is tossed, heads has probability 1.

✓ Correct Answer: False. Heads has probability 1/2.

Q21. Experimental probability can change when an experiment is repeated a different number of times.

✓ Correct Answer: True. Experimental results depend on the actual outcomes observed.

Q22. If a bag contains no green balls, the probability of drawing a green ball is 1.

✓ Correct Answer: False. It is an impossible event, so the probability is 0.

Q23. A probability of 1/2 can describe an event that is equally likely to happen or not happen.

✓ Correct Answer: True.

Q24. If an event has probability 3/4, its complement has probability 1/4.

✓ Correct Answer: True. Together an event and its complement have probability 1.

Q25. Rolling a 7 on a standard six-sided die is a certain event.

✓ Correct Answer: False. It is impossible.

Section C — Assertion & Reasoning

A = Both A and R are true and R explains A
B = Both A and R are true but R does not explain A
C = A is true but R is false
D = A is false but R is true

Q26. Assertion: The probability of getting a 6 on a fair die is 1/6.
Reason: A fair die has six equally likely outcomes.

✓ Correct Answer: A. Both statements are true, and six equally likely outcomes explain the probability 1/6.

Q27. Assertion: An impossible event has probability 0.
Reason: An impossible event has no favourable outcome.

✓ Correct Answer: A. Both are true, and having no favourable outcome explains probability 0.

Q28. Assertion: The probability of getting an odd number on a fair die is 1/2.
Reason: There are three odd numbers among the six faces.

✓ Correct Answer: A. The odd faces are 1, 3 and 5, so 3/6 = 1/2.

Q29. Assertion: A probability of 5/4 is possible for an event.
Reason: Probability lies between 0 and 1 inclusive.

✓ Correct Answer: D. The assertion is false because 5/4 is greater than 1. The reason is true.

Q30. Assertion: In a fair coin toss, heads and tails are equally likely.
Reason: A fair coin has two possible outcomes.

✓ Correct Answer: B. Both statements are true, but merely having two outcomes does not guarantee equal likelihood; fairness does.

Section G — HOTS / Higher Order Thinking

Q46. A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue and 3 green balls. One ball is chosen at random. What is the probability that it is either red or green?

✓ Correct Answer: 7/12. Red or green = 4 + 3 = 7 favourable balls out of 12.

Q47. A fair die is rolled twice. Which of the following describes the probability of getting a 6 at least once?

✓ Correct Answer: 11/36. Probability of no 6 in both rolls is 25/36, so 1 − 25/36 = 11/36.

Q48. A spinner has 8 equal sections: 3 red, 2 blue, 2 green and 1 yellow. Which colour has the greatest probability of being selected?

✓ Correct Answer: Red. Red occupies 3 of the 8 equal sections, more than any other colour.

Q49. A coin is tossed 50 times. Heads occur 28 times. If the same experiment is performed again, which statement is most reasonable?

✓ Correct Answer: The number of heads may be different from 28. Experimental results can vary from trial to trial.

Q50. A box contains 2 white, 4 black and 4 orange counters. Without looking, one counter is chosen. Which colour is twice as likely to be selected as white?

✓ Correct Answer: Black and Orange. White has 2 counters, while each of Black and Orange has 4 counters, so each is twice as likely as white.

Q51. A bag contains 4 white, 2 black and 6 red balls. What is the probability of selecting a ball that is not red?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/2. Non-red balls = 4 + 2 = 6 out of 12, giving 6/12 = 1/2.

Q52. A number is selected randomly from 1 to 20. What is the probability that it is a factor of 20?

✓ Correct Answer: 3/10. Factors of 20 from 1 to 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10 and 20: 6/20 = 3/10.

Q53. Two fair coins are tossed together. What is the probability of getting exactly one head?

✓ Correct Answer: 1/2. Outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT. Exactly one head occurs in HT and TH: 2/4 = 1/2.

Q54. A spinner has 12 equal sections. Five are blue and the rest are yellow. How many blue sections would be needed to make the probability of blue equal to 1/2?

✓ Correct Answer: 6. Half of 12 equal sections is 6.

Q55. A game awards a point when a die shows an even number. Riya plays 30 rounds and scores 17 points. Her friend says, “The probability of scoring a point is exactly 1/2.” Is this statement correct for the experimental probability?

✓ Correct Answer: No, the experimental probability is 17/30. It is based on the actual number of successful rounds.
